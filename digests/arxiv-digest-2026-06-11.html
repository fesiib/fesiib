⭐ Picks

⭐ Nonslop: A Gamified Experiment in Human-AI Collaborative Writing

  • Authors: Maria Edwards, Julian Togelius
  • Primary category: cs.AI
  • Why it matters: A human-subject study of AI-assisted writing that asks when people accept suggestions and how that affects individual voice. Very aligned with creativity support and human-AI co-creation: it treats adoption of AI text as an interaction phenomenon, not just a generation-quality problem.

⭐ PROJECTMEM: A Local-First, Event-Sourced Memory and Judgment Layer for AI Coding Agents

  • Authors: Ripon Chandra Malo, Tong Qiu
  • Primary category: cs.AI
  • Why it matters: Proposes a local-first, event-sourced memory and judgment layer for AI coding agents so project decisions and failed attempts persist across sessions. This is directly relevant to agent interfaces and “working memory” for long-running AI collaboration.

⭐ Natural-Language Temporal Grounding in Hour-Long Videos is a Search Problem: A Benchmark and Empirical Decomposition

  • Authors: Sukmin Seo, Geewook Kim
  • Primary category: cs.CV
  • Why it matters: Frames hour-long natural-language video temporal grounding as a search problem, with a benchmark and decomposition of where systems fail. Strong fit for multimodal interaction and video editing/search interfaces, especially for long-form media workflows.

⭐ Agentic Environment Engineering for Large Language Models: A Survey of Environment Modeling, Synthesis, Evaluation, and Application

  • Authors: Jiachun Li, Zhuoran Jin, Tianyi Men et al.
  • Primary category: cs.CL
  • Why it matters: A survey of agentic environments: modeling, synthesis, evaluation, and applications for LLM agents. Useful as a map of how agents learn and are tested through interactive systems rather than static prompts.

⭐ Somewhere Over the Desktop: A Research Agenda for Ubiquitous Analytics

  • Authors: Niklas Elmqvist, Panagiotis D. Ritsos, Peter W. S. Butcher
  • Primary category: cs.HC
  • Why it matters: A research agenda for ubiquitous analytics in the era of spatial computing, generative AI, open web standards, and agentic AI. This is very HCI-facing: it asks what analytics interfaces become when distributed across headsets, smartglasses, rooms, and AI agents.

⭐ Toward Generalist Autonomous Research via Hypothesis-Tree Refinement

  • Authors: Jiajie Jin, Yuyang Hu, Kai Qiu et al.
  • Primary category: cs.CL
  • Why it matters: Introduces Arbor, a hypothesis-tree framework for autonomous research agents that iteratively explore, experiment, and carry forward lessons. Interesting for LLM agents and scientific workflows because it models research as long-horizon refinement rather than one-shot idea generation.

⭐ Designing AI-Supported Focus Groups: A Role x Modality Playbook

  • Authors: Zhiqing Wang, Steven Dow
  • Primary category: cs.HC
  • Why it matters: Turns AI-supported focus groups into a role × modality playbook, targeting moderation, participant sensemaking, and design research practice. Strong human-AI interaction paper because it studies where AI should sit in a sensitive group-discussion workflow.

⭐ WorldReasoner: Evaluating Whether Language Model Agents Forecast Events with Valid Reasoning

  • Authors: Yizhou Chi, Eric Chamoun, Zifeng Ding et al.
  • Primary category: cs.CL
  • Why it matters: WorldReasoner evaluates whether forecasting agents reason validly, not merely whether final predictions are right. This is useful for agent evaluation: it separates supported causal reasoning from memorization, fabricated evidence, or lucky answers.

⭐ AnchorEdit: Maintaining Temporal Consistency in Multi-turn Image Editing via Causal Memory

  • Authors: Hang Xu, Xiaoxiao Ma, Guohui Zhang et al.
  • Primary category: cs.CV
  • Why it matters: AnchorEdit targets multi-turn image editing, using causal memory to reduce identity drift and error accumulation. Directly relevant to interactive creative tools where users iteratively refine media over several turns.

⭐ Hey Chat, Can You Teach Me? Structuring Socratic Dialogue for Human Learning in the Wild

  • Authors: Sidney Tio, Arunesh Sinha, Pradeep Varakantham
  • Primary category: cs.CL
  • Why it matters: Studies Socratic dialogue for learning “in the wild,” where an LLM has to infer a learner’s knowledge from ordinary chat rather than a formal LMS record. Highly aligned with Bekzat’s LLMs × education interests.

⭐ Substrate Asymmetry in User-Side Memory: A Diagnostic Framework

  • Authors: Youwang Deng
  • Primary category: cs.CL
  • Why it matters: Argues that user-side memory should be decomposed into behavioral consistency, factual presence, and preference force instead of collapsed into one personalization score. Useful framing for evaluating memory in personal agents and educational assistants.

⭐ Goal-Autopilot: A Verifiable Anti-Fabrication Firewall for Unattended Long-Horizon Agents

  • Authors: Youwang Deng
  • Primary category: cs.CL
  • Why it matters: Goal-Autopilot proposes a verifiable anti-fabrication firewall for unattended long-horizon agents, bounding what agents may claim at termination. Very relevant to trust, control, and verification for agentic systems.

⭐ Organize then Retrieve: Hierarchical Memory Navigation for Efficient Agents

  • Authors: Hao-Lun Hsu, Nikki Lijing Kuang, Boyi Liu et al.
  • Primary category: cs.AI
  • Why it matters: Presents hierarchical memory navigation for efficient agents: organize first, then retrieve. Strong fit for long-horizon agent design because it addresses context growth, latency, and degraded reasoning through structured working memory.

⭐ Learning by Chatting? Investigating the Impact of Generative AI on Information Seeking and Learning

  • Authors: Shravika Mittal, Su Lin Blodgett, Q. Vera Liao
  • Primary category: cs.HC
  • Why it matters: A field experiment on GenAI-mediated information seeking and learning. Directly relevant to education/HCI because it studies whether chat-based search changes learning outcomes, not just user satisfaction.

⭐ SkillJuror: Measuring How Agent Skill Organization Changes Runtime Behavior

  • Authors: Zhiyu Chen, Zihan Guo, Bo Huang et al.
  • Primary category: cs.AI
  • Why it matters: SkillJuror measures how the organization of agent skills changes runtime behavior, contrasting progressive disclosure with flattened skill instructions. This is unusually close to practical agent-interface design and skill/tool packaging.
